## Every formula in this course, in one place

Meant to be useful after you finish the course, not just during it. Each
entry links back conceptually to the module that built it — if a formula
here doesn't immediately make sense, that module is where it came from.

### Stage 1 — Vectors

$$\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b} = a_1b_1 + a_2b_2 \qquad \|\mathbf{a}\| = \sqrt{a_1^2+a_2^2} \qquad \cos\theta = \frac{\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}}{\|\mathbf{a}\|\|\mathbf{b}\|}$$

Dot product, magnitude, and cosine similarity — direction-only comparison
between two vectors.

### Stage 2 — Matrices

$$M\mathbf{v} = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} \qquad \det(M) = ad-bc$$

A matrix as a linear transformation; its determinant as the area-scaling
factor, and the test for invertibility ($\det=0$ means no inverse).

### Stage 3 — Eigenvectors and PCA

$$M\mathbf{v} = \lambda\mathbf{v} \qquad \det(M-\lambda I) = 0 \qquad \lambda = \frac{\operatorname{tr}(M)}{2}\pm\sqrt{\left(\frac{\operatorname{tr}(M)}{2}\right)^2-\det(M)}$$

Eigenvectors are directions a transformation only scales, never rotates;
for PCA, they're the principal directions of a covariance matrix.

### Stage 4 — Calculus and Gradients

$$\nabla f = \left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}, \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\right)$$

The gradient always points toward the steepest increase.

### Stage 5 — Optimization

$$\mathbf{x}_{n+1} = \mathbf{x}_n - \eta\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_n)$$

Gradient descent: step opposite the gradient, scaled by the learning rate
$\eta$. Too large an $\eta$ causes oscillation or divergence.

### Stage 6 — Probability

$$\mathbb{E}[X] = p \qquad \operatorname{Var}(X) = p(1-p) \qquad \operatorname{SE} = \sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(X)/n}$$

Expectation, variance, and standard error — why averages stabilize as $n$
grows (the law of large numbers).

### Stage 7 — Statistics

$$\bar{X}_n \sim \mathcal{N}\!\left(\mu, \frac{\sigma^2}{n}\right)\ \text{as } n\to\infty$$

The Central Limit Theorem: sample means trend toward a normal distribution
regardless of the population's own shape.

### Stage 8 — Likelihood

$$\log\mathcal{L}(\mu,\sigma) = \sum_{i=1}^n \log p(x_i\mid\mu,\sigma)$$

Maximum likelihood estimation: choose parameters that make the observed
data most plausible. For a Gaussian, the MLE is the sample mean and
sample standard deviation.

### Stage 9 — Information and Loss

$$H(p) = -\sum_i p_i\log_2 p_i \qquad H(p,q) = -\sum_i p_i\log_2 q_i \qquad D_{KL}(p\|q) = H(p,q)-H(p)$$

Entropy, cross-entropy, and KL divergence — the mathematical basis of most
classifier loss functions.

### Stage 10 — Neural Network Mathematics

$$z = w_1x_1+w_2x_2+b \qquad a=\sigma(z) \qquad \frac{\partial L}{\partial w_1} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial a}\cdot\frac{\partial a}{\partial z}\cdot\frac{\partial z}{\partial w_1}$$

Forward pass, then the chain rule backward through it — backpropagation in
miniature.

### Stage 11 — Attention

$$\text{weight}_i = \frac{e^{(q\cdot k_i)/\sqrt{d}}}{\sum_j e^{(q\cdot k_j)/\sqrt{d}}} \qquad \text{output} = \sum_i \text{weight}_i\cdot v_i$$

Scaled dot-product attention: relevance via dot product, normalized by
softmax, combined as a weighted sum of values.

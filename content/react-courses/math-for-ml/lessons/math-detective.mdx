## Not every confident-sounding claim is correct

Machine learning is full of plausible-sounding statements that are subtly
wrong, or true only under specific conditions nobody mentioned. This is
where you practice catching that — not by answering a multiple-choice
question, but by investigating a claim the way you'd actually need to in a
real job: figure out exactly which parts are true, which parts are false,
and what the honestly-corrected version should say.

<Callout tone="info" label="How this works">
Each case has two phases. **Phase 1** gives you a "charge sheet" — several
statements related to the claim. You have to correctly flag every TRUE one
and leave every FALSE one unchecked — no partial credit, same discipline
as a real investigation. **Phase 2** unlocks once you get Phase 1 exactly
right: pick the accurately-qualified version of the original claim, not a
flat denial and not an overcorrection.
</Callout>

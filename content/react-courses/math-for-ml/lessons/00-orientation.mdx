## Welcome

If you're intimidated by the mathematics behind machine learning, you're
exactly who this course is for. You don't need calculus, linear algebra,
or statistics going in - you need curiosity and a willingness to drag a
slider and see what happens before you worry about the formula underneath
it. By the end of the course, you'll be able to look at an equation, a
model, or a training curve and reason about what's actually happening
mathematically, not just recognize that it "looks like machine learning
math."

<Callout tone="info" label="Who this course assumes you are">
Someone who can read and follow careful English explanations, is
comfortable with basic arithmetic and algebra (solving for x, working with
fractions), and has never needed calculus or linear algebra for anything
serious. If parts of a module feel like review, skim faster - nothing
here is timed, and the point is understanding, not attendance.
</Callout>

## How this course actually works

Every module follows the same shape, on purpose - once you know the
pattern, you can move faster through it each time:

1. **Intuition first.** A plain-English explanation before any notation.
2. **A real interactive lab.** Something you drag, click, or adjust - with
   genuine computation running underneath it, not a canned animation.
3. **A prediction prompt.** You guess what will happen before you look -
   this is where real learning happens, not in reading the answer.
4. **The formal math**, via the **Show Me the Math** toggle: three levels
   - *Intuition* (no notation), *Applied Math* (the formula, explained
   symbol by symbol), and *Full Derivation* (where the formula actually
   comes from, for when you want to go deeper). You can stay on level 1
   for the whole course and still finish it - the labs and the checkpoint
   quizzes don't require the derivations.
5. **Where it shows up in real ML** - so the math never floats free of
   the reason you're learning it.
6. **A failure mode it prevents** - a real, specific mistake this idea
   stops you from making.
7. **A checkpoint quiz** - five questions with full explanations for every
   wrong answer, not just "incorrect."

## The roadmap

The course builds in one direction: each stage's math becomes an
ingredient in the next one, culminating in a real neural network and the
attention mechanism behind modern language models.

<Callout tone="info" label="All 11 stages, in order">
**Stage 1 - Thinking in Vectors.** Vectors, dot products, cosine
similarity - the mathematical basis of "how similar are two things."

**Stage 2 - Transforming Data.** Matrices as functions that move points
around; determinants; what it means for a transformation to be reversible.

**Stage 3 - Finding the Important Directions.** Eigenvectors, eigenvalues,
and PCA - the course's signature lab.

**Stage 4 - Mathematics of Change.** Derivatives, partial derivatives, and
the gradient - "which way is uphill."

**Stage 5 - Optimization.** Gradient descent, learning rate, and what
convergence, oscillation, and divergence actually look like.

**Stage 6 - Probability.** Expectation, variance, and the law of large
numbers, built from a single biased coin.

**Stage 7 - Statistics.** Population vs. sample, and the Central Limit
Theorem, demonstrated with real random sampling.

**Stage 8 - Probability Meets ML.** Likelihood and maximum likelihood
estimation - why common loss functions look the way they do.

**Stage 9 - Information and Loss.** Entropy, cross-entropy, and KL
divergence - building up to the formula behind most classifier training.

**Stage 10 - Deep Learning Mathematics.** One real neuron, traced
completely: forward pass, loss, backpropagation, and a real weight update.

**Stage 11 - The Math Behind Attention.** Every previous idea, combined
into the mechanism behind transformers.
</Callout>

## A note on pacing

Go "hard little by little" - that's a real, deliberate design choice, not
a compromise. Every module's Level 1 (Intuition) explanation is enough to
follow the course start to finish. If a module's Applied Math or Full
Derivation level feels like too much on a first pass, that's fine - move
on, and come back to it later once you've seen how the idea gets used
downstream. Understanding tends to arrive in layers, not all at once, and
this course is built to let it.

When you're ready, start with Module 1.

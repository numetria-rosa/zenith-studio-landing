<ProdWarn title={"What would break in production?"}>

- **Stale documents with no recency signal.** A superseded FAQ page scores well on pure semantic similarity, embeddings don't know a newer page exists unless you tell them.
- **Duplicate or near-duplicate chunks.** The same fact indexed twice fills two of your k slots with the same information instead of two distinct, useful pieces of context.
- **Confident answers on thin evidence.** A 0.4-score chunk is still a chunk, and a model asked to answer from context will usually try, whether or not that context actually supports a confident claim.
- **No threshold at all.** Without a relevance floor, "nothing in the collection is actually relevant" and "the best match happens to be great" look identical to the retrieval step.

<Rule>

**Rule of thumb:** a retrieval system should be able to say "I don't have relevant information for that." A system that always returns k results, no matter how weak, can't.

</Rule>

- Distinguish retryable from non-retryable failures, and explain why the distinction matters.
- Explain exponential backoff with full jitter and the retry-storm problem it solves.
- Explain why fallback should be attempted once, not retried with the same budget as the primary.
- Implement a call wrapper with bounded retries and a single bounded fallback attempt.

<ProdWarn title={"What would break in production?"}>

- **Retries causing duplicate actions.** A retried call without an idempotency key (Module 4) can re-run a side effect that already succeeded the first time.
- **Retry storms.** Every client retrying at the same fixed interval synchronizes into waves that hammer an already-struggling service right as it's trying to recover.
- **Backoff with no cap.** Doubling delay forever eventually means a "retry" that happens minutes after the request was actually useful, effectively a silent failure with extra steps.
- **Silent failures.** A fallback that also fails and gets swallowed instead of surfaced looks, from the outside, identical to a system that's working fine.

<Rule>

**Rule of thumb:** retry logic is not "try again," it's a policy with four parts: what's retryable, how long to wait, how many times, and what happens when every attempt is exhausted. Missing any one part is a production incident waiting for the right kind of bad day.

</Rule>

</ProdWarn>

<Muted>A short consolidation before you're graded on it.</Muted>

- Describe the reason-act-observe cycle and why the model, not your code, decides each step's content.
- Explain why a step limit is required regardless of how capable the model is.
- Distinguish success, step-limit, and stuck as three separate, differently-handled outcomes.
- Implement a loop that enforces a step limit and detects repeated-action stalls.

<ProdWarn title={"What would break in production?"}>

- **No step limit.** "The model will know when to stop" is a hope, not a control mechanism, an unbounded loop is an unbounded bill, in tokens, tool calls, and time.
- **Tool abuse from an unconstrained loop.** A stuck agent doesn't just waste steps, if one of its tools has a real side effect, a stuck loop can repeat that side effect just as easily as it repeats a harmless search.
- **Incorrect task decomposition treated as success.** A loop that hits its step limit with plausible-looking partial reasoning can look done from the outside. Scraping the last reasoning text for something answer-shaped is not the same as the model actually finishing.
- **Runaway cost from a legitimate-looking loop.** Twenty different tool calls, no repeats, still hits the same bill as twenty repeated ones if nothing bounds the loop itself, distinct actions aren't automatically cheap actions.

<Rule>

**Rule of thumb:** a loop needs a hard step limit AND stuck-detection, because they catch two different failures, running out of budget on a genuinely hard task, and spinning on a task that's actually broken.

</Rule>

</ProdWarn>

<Muted>A short consolidation before you're graded on it.</Muted>

- Estimate token counts for planning, and compute available input budget from context window minus reserved output.
- Explain why naive truncation is a silent-failure bug, not a correctness shortcut.
- Explain what overlap buys you at a chunk boundary, and that it isn't free.
- Implement a chunker that never silently drops content, even for an oversized single unit.

<ProdWarn title={"What would break in production?"}>

- **Truncation with no strategy.** Cutting off at the limit silently discards whatever came after, no error, no warning, just a wrong answer that looks confident.
- **Fixed chunk sizes across document types.** A chunk size tuned for short support tickets splits a legal contract's clauses mid-sentence.
- **Overlap tuned to zero.** Saves tokens right up until the one fact you needed was split exactly across a boundary and appears in neither chunk intact.
- **Budget math that forgets reserved output.** Computing available input as the full context window, with no room saved for the response, produces a request that fails at generation time, not before it.

<Rule>

**Rule of thumb:** a token budget is arithmetic you do before the call, not a limit you discover from an error message after it.

</Rule>

</ProdWarn>

<Connection title={"The engineering connection"}>

You can now fit content into a budget without silently losing any of it. But chunking solves "how do I fit content," not "which chunk is actually relevant to this specific question." Module 3 is retrieval, the mechanism that searches across chunks instead of hoping the right one made it into the prompt by luck.

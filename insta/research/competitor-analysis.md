## 1. THE DOMINANT MECHANIC: "Comment-to-DM" lead magnets

Almost every high-performing post in this niche uses the same funnel, regardless of account size:

> Caption teases a resource (repo list, roadmap, prompt pack, checklist) → "Comment [KEYWORD] and
> I'll DM you the link" → hundreds of low-effort keyword comments (pure engagement-bait, but it
> works) → creator DMs the link, which is usually a lead magnet into their email list/product.

Examples seen: anurag.builds ("SKILLS"), artem.novitckii ("TOKENS"), the.natasha.ai ("Roadmap"),
lior ("CEO" — used on at least 2 separate posts, evergreen), piyush.glitch ("free"), seb.ai (used
"POST", "COMPANY", "TEAM" across different posts — same template, different topic each time),
buildwithneej ("GITHUB"), aspirenest0b9 ("WHATSAPP"), jackroberts___ ("CLAUDE", "JARVIS"),
ibraviz.ai ("LIST"/"listen" typo-variant), growthwithhardik ("CAROUSEL"), buildercult
("unhinged").

**Why it works:** the comment is a near-zero-friction micro-commitment (Instagram's algorithm also
rewards comment volume), and it forces every interested viewer into the creator's DMs — a much
higher-intent list than link-in-bio clickers.

**Direct application to zenith-studio:** every course/service ad should end with a comment-keyword
CTA, not just "link in bio." E.g. "Comment 'AI' and I'll send you the syllabus" instead of a plain
link. This is the single highest-leverage tactic across all 26 samples.

## 2. Hook formula (from kallawaymarketing, explicit breakdown)

Two posts from `kallawaymarketing` are meta-content that literally teach the hook formulas being
used everywhere else in this research set:

**Triple Hook Method** (reel, DbfnLfgP40k):
1. **Context** — first line/visual must establish topic immediately (delay = higher skip rate)
2. **Lean** — make the viewer curious enough to want more ("opens the curiosity loop")
3. **Contrarian Snapback** — say something that pulls against the direction they were leaning
   (like hooking a fish: pull one way, then jerk back)

**Dopamine Addiction Loop** (reel, DZyyYCJuCal):
1. **Stakes** — a character to root for + something at risk + urgency
2. **The Big Question** — enough context to plant a specific, juicy question in the viewer's mind
3. **The Headfake** — contrast against what the viewer expects/believes
4. **The Rehook** — restart the loop again immediately (great content has many cascading loops)

**Application:** use these as a literal checklist when writing reel scripts for zenith-studio's AI
services — don't just state what the service does, open a curiosity gap first.

## 3. Design styles worth stealing

### 3a. Bold poster / editorial style — `artem.novitckii` (Dbx1P3FlED4)
"CLAUDE IS EATING YOUR TOKENS." Cream background, thick black condensed headline type, one
saturated accent color (burnt orange) as a color-block behind the punch line, a 3D-rendered
"lego-style" mascot icon, thin corner crop-marks (like a museum label/print plate) framing the
whole card. Reads like a gallery print, not a "social media post." **This is the strongest single
design in the set** — worth directly emulating for a course-ad template.

### 3b. Cinematic photo + bold overlay type — `sociyell` (DctnnfmAWbC, Dcy2EgTgac6)
Real aerial/drone photography (tennis court from directly above, or a fashion-editorial pink-fur
photo) with oversized white sans headline text stacked directly on top, one word in a different
weight/style (italic serif mixed with bold sans) for contrast. Very "high-production ad," not
"canva template." Feels premium and stops the scroll specifically because it doesn't look like
typical AI-account content.

### 3c. Minimalist terminal/editorial — `lior` (Db06eoekZtO)
Cream/off-white background, monospace or condensed serif type, small line-icon, understated
color (one thin underline accent), lots of whitespace. "10 STARTUPS THAT DON'T EXIST YET (BUT NEED
TO IN 2026)." Reads like a magazine pull-quote. Works because of restraint — no gradients, no
stock 3D icons.

### 3d. Diagram/pill-icon infographic — `seb.ai` (DcQLf71Fe7k, Dc4wfCAGOxc)
Cream background, black + one accent color (orange or green) headline, a horizontal row of
rounded "pill" icons each labeled with one workflow step (PRODUCTS → LEADS → CONTENT → SALES →
...). This is a repeatable *template* — same layout, new icons/labels per post. Good candidate to
copy for an "our AI service pipeline" explainer carousel.

### 3e. Roadmap carousel — `the.natasha.ai` (Dc6MI9ik9za)
Cream background, orange/rust accent, dotted-line roadmap with circular waypoints and a flag at
the end ("7 steps · from zero to agent builder"). Clean, systematic, screenshot-and-save bait.

**Cross-cutting design pattern:** cream/off-white backgrounds + one saturated accent color + bold
condensed sans headline shows up in 5+ of the best-performing posts. Pure black backgrounds with
neon/gradient text (buildwithneej, growthwithhardik) also perform but read more "generic AI
account" — the cream-background editorial look is the differentiator.

## 6c. LinkedIn + Claude automation — full tip breakdown (from `aiwithanushka`, Dc8_6F0CAI4)

Directly relevant to zenith-studio's own future LinkedIn automation plans — full carousel notes:

1. **There's no official LinkedIn connector in Claude** (unlike Gmail/Drive) — you connect it
   through a **custom MCP connector** you bring yourself. This means the tool you pick determines
   your risk, not Claude itself.
2. **Safe vs. bannable, explicitly split:**
   - Safe (official API): draft posts in your voice, schedule to your page, analyze what performed,
     research and repurpose, summarize your own inbox.
   - Risky (bots/scraping): mass connection requests, automated DM sequences, scraping profiles in
     bulk, browser-extension bots, auto-commenting at scale.
   - LinkedIn's User Agreement Section 8.2 explicitly bars bots/automated activity — accounts get
     restricted.
3. **This isn't hypothetical** — cites LinkedIn acting against automation vendor HeyReach in March
   2026, ~30,000 users losing LinkedIn automation overnight, and 2026 enforcement skipping warnings
   and going straight to suspension. Takeaway line: "Your profile is an asset you can't rebuild."
4. **Safe use #1 — drafts that sound like you:** feed Claude your best posts, get drafts back in
   your own voice; you review, then post or schedule yourself (human stays in the loop).
5. **Safe use #2 — read your last 30 posts, not just write the next one:** ask Claude what patterns
   drove performance (e.g. "hooks with a number → 2.1x impressions," "under 900 characters → 1.6x
   saves," "Tuesday 9am → best reach every week"). **The reading was the bottleneck, not the
   writing** — analyze before generating.
6. **Setup, 4 steps:** pick an official-API content platform (not a browser bot) → connect your
   LinkedIn profile/page to it → copy its MCP URL (contains your API key — treat it like a
   password, never paste it in a post or screenshot) → in Claude, Settings → Connectors → Add
   custom connector.
7. **5 starter prompts worth stealing verbatim:**
   - "Find the 3 patterns in my top posts this quarter."
   - "Draft 5 posts from this transcript, in my voice."
   - "Rewrite this hook 6 ways and tell me which is strongest."
   - "Turn my last newsletter into a LinkedIn carousel outline."
   - "Schedule these three for Tuesday mornings."
8. **The line that matters:** "Use Claude to think, write, and analyze. Not to fake being human at
   scale. That's the line, and it's where accounts die."

**Application:** when we build zenith-studio's own LinkedIn presence/automation, use an official-API
content platform (not scraping/bot tools) as the actual LinkedIn connector, keep a human-review
step before anything posts, and use the "analyze your own last 30 posts" prompt pattern before
generating new content — same principle as the sociyell reel case study in section 4.

### 6c-ii. Same creator — landing international clients with AI (DcakHVciG28)

Companion carousel, same account (`aiwithanushka`), same practical-tactics format. Directly
relevant to zenith-studio pitching AI services (law firms, real estate, etc.) to clients outside
Tunisia. Full tactic breakdown:

- **The myth that keeps you local:** a foreign address or agency brand isn't needed, neither is a
  network you don't have yet — what wins is proof, packaging, and response time. "They are not
  buying your country. They are buying a solved problem." Their own first international client
  (Dubai) came from a post, not a cold email.
- **Tactic 1 — One niche, one outcome:** write the offer as a *result*, not a service — "AI
  consulting for businesses" is invisible; "AI automations for travel agencies" is findable, with
  a measurable outcome attached ("14 hrs/week saved"). Narrow is what makes you findable from
  thousands of km away.
- **Tactic 2 — Build the prospect list with AI:** use a research agent to pull a list (e.g. "50
  agencies in Dubai"), scan their actual booking/intake flow, and write one concrete signal per
  company (not scraped emails — gathered evidence of a specific problem). ~6 hours of manual
  research compresses to ~40 minutes.
- **Tactic 2b — Cut fifty down to ten:** delete every row where the signal is generic (that's
  noise, not a lead) and manually open each site yourself before pitching — AI invents pages that
  don't exist, so unverified research produces false leads.
- **Tactic 3 — Personalize so they believe it:** AI drafts the *observation* (what's broken on
  their site/flow); the human writes the actual offer. Same pitch, but the tone shifts per market
  — direct for US, understated for UK, formal for Gulf clients.
- **Tactic 4 — Send a 3-minute audit, not a pitch:** a short screen-recording teardown of the
  prospect's actual booking/intake flow, diagnosing the problem for free. "You give away the
  diagnosis. They pay for the fix." Converts far better than a cold DM because it's demonstrated
  work, not a claim.
- **Tactic 5 — Reply with a same-day proposal:** price in the client's local currency, not your
  own converted. Speed reads as competence — most competitors take a week to reply.
- **Tactic 6 — Content is the real funnel (slowest, but compounds):** track where leads actually
  come from — for this creator, "Posts" outperformed cold DMs, referrals, and audits combined.
  Every completed project becomes a post: the problem, what was built, the number it moved.
- **The unglamorous part nobody posts:** sort payment rails and know your export/invoicing
  position *before* the first invoice — get paid isn't automatic across borders. Get every scope
  in a written contract before work starts. And the real moat is reply speed, not your tool stack
  — a different timezone is a selling point (always-on coverage), not a liability.

**Application:** this is a ready-made outreach playbook for pitching zenith-studio's AI services
(Law Firm AI Team, Brokerage AI Team) to clients abroad — narrow niche positioning with a
measurable outcome, a free diagnostic audit as the actual pitch mechanism instead of cold outreach,
and treating case-study posts as the primary lead-gen channel rather than an afterthought.

## 6. Recommended synthesis for zenith-studio's AI-course/AI-services content

1. **Every post ends with a comment-keyword CTA**, not just "link in bio."
2. **Default visual template:** cream/off-white background, one bold accent color, thick condensed
   headline type, minimal iconography — matches artem.novitckii/lior/seb.ai's best performers and
   differentiates from generic black-gradient "AI account" aesthetic.
3. **For explaining AI services to non-technical buyers (law firms, real estate — per the user's
   own note on DcbC1wBFFfI/similar):** use the seb.ai pill-diagram format — one horizontal pipeline
   graphic showing "your problem → our AI agents → your result," reusable per-service.
4. **For reels:** open with a specific number or a case-study before/after (sociyell's format),
   never a generic explainer intro.
5. **Hook checklist before publishing anything:** does line 1 give immediate context? Does it open
   a curiosity gap? Is there a contrarian turn before the payoff? (kallawaymarketing's Triple Hook)
